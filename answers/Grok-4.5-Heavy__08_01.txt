**Yes.**

One concrete firing sequence from the initial marking is:

**T1 → T2 → T5**

- Start: \(p_0=1\), all others 0.  
- Fire T1: consumes \(p_0\), produces tokens in \(p_1\) and \(p_2\). Marking: \(p_1=1\), \(p_2=1\).  
- Fire T2: consumes \(p_1\), produces a token in \(p_3\). Marking: \(p_2=1\), \(p_3=1\) (and \(p_4=0\)).  
- T5 is now enabled: it has a token on the ordinary arc from \(p_3\) and the inhibitor arc from \(p_4\) is satisfied because \(p_4\) is empty. Firing T5 consumes the token from \(p_3\) and produces a token in \(p_6\).  

At the moment T5 fires, T3 has never occurred (the token that would enable T3 is still sitting unused in \(p_2\)).  

Thus T5 can (and does) fire before T3 in a reachable marking.
